## Vitamin D3 (Cholecalciferol)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Active Form:** Cholecalciferol (Vitamin D3)
- **Inactive Form:** Ergocalciferol (Vitamin D2) - less bioavailable
- **Metabolites:** 25(OH)D (calcidiol), 1,25(OH)2D (calcitriol - active hormone)

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:** 20 μg/day (800 IU)
- **Age-specific:**
  - Adults (18-74 years): 10 μg/day (400 IU)
  - Adults (>75 years): 20 μg/day (800 IU)
  - Pregnant/lactating: 10 μg/day (400 IU)

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General population:** 1,000-2,000 IU/day (25-50 μg)
- **Deficiency correction:** 2,000-4,000 IU/day (50-100 μg)
- **Target serum level:** 75-125 nmol/L (30-50 ng/mL)
- **Clinical evidence:**
  - [2025 meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1505616/full): 400-1200 IU/day reduces respiratory infections
  - Dose-response for depression: -0.32 SMD per 1000 IU/day

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50 (animal models):** >10,000 IU/kg (extremely high, unlikely in humans)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 4,000 IU/day (100 μg) for adults
- **Toxicity symptoms:** Hypercalcemia (>150 nmol/L serum), nausea, vomiting, kidney stones
- **Chronic toxicity threshold:** >10,000 IU/day for extended periods

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** Morning with breakfast (fat-containing meal)
- **Rationale:** Fat-soluble vitamin, requires dietary fat for absorption; morning aligns with natural circadian synthesis

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin K2 (MK-7):** 100-200 μg - directs calcium to bones (not arteries)
- **Magnesium:** 200-400 mg - required for Vitamin D metabolism (activates 25-hydroxylase)
- **Boron:** 3-6 mg - enhances Vitamin D activity
- **Dietary fat:** 10-15g fat with meal (increases absorption by 50%)

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **High-dose Vitamin A (retinol):** >10,000 IU competes for receptor binding
- **Corticosteroids:** Prednisone reduces Vitamin D absorption

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **Thiazide diuretics:** Risk of hypercalcemia (monitor calcium levels)
- **Cardiac glycosides (Digoxin):** Hypercalcemia increases toxicity risk
- **Anticonvulsants (Phenytoin, Carbamazepine):** Increase Vitamin D metabolism (may need higher doses)
- **Orlistat (weight loss drug):** Reduces fat-soluble vitamin absorption by 30%
- **Cholestyramine:** Binds Vitamin D, reducing absorption

### Genetic Considerations (VDR Gene)
- **VDR Fok1 polymorphism (ff genotype):** May require 25-50% higher doses for same serum levels
- **VDR Bsm1/Taq1 polymorphisms:** Associated with lower bone density, may benefit from higher doses

## Methylfolate (5-MTHF / L-Methylfolate)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Active Form:** L-5-Methyltetrahydrofolate (L-5-MTHF, Metafolin®, Quatrefolic®)
- **Synthetic Form:** Folic acid (requires enzymatic conversion via MTHFR)
- **Natural Food Form:** Folate (found in leafy greens)

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:** 400 μg DFE (Dietary Folate Equivalents)
- **Pregnant women:** 600 μg DFE
- **Lactating women:** 500 μg DFE

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **MTHFR C677T heterozygotes (CT):** 600-800 μg methylfolate
- **MTHFR C677T homozygotes (TT):** 800-1,000 μg methylfolate
- **Cardiovascular health:** 800 μg (lowers homocysteine by 25%)
- **Clinical evidence:**
  - [2023 RCT](https://www.clinicalnutritionespen.com/article/S2405-4577(23)01232-9/fulltext): Methylfolate equivalent to folic acid for homocysteine reduction
  - Depression adjunct therapy: 15 mg/day L-methylfolate (medical dose)

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Not established (extremely low toxicity)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 1,000 μg/day for folic acid (no UL for natural folate/methylfolate)
- **Toxicity concerns:** Masking of B12 deficiency at high folic acid doses (not a concern with methylfolate)
- **Safe upper range:** Up to 5,000 μg methylfolate studied without adverse effects

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** Morning with or without food
- **Rationale:** Water-soluble, does not require fat; morning supports methylation cycle and neurotransmitter synthesis

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin B12 (Methylcobalamin):** 500-1,000 μg - works synergistically in methylation cycle
- **Vitamin B6 (P5P):** 10-25 mg - supports homocysteine metabolism
- **Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin):** 10-25 mg - MTHFR enzyme cofactor (especially for C677T variants)
- **Zinc:** 15-30 mg - supports folate metabolism

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **High-dose folic acid:** >400 μg may compete for absorption (use methylfolate instead)
- **Alcohol:** Impairs folate absorption and metabolism

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **Methotrexate (chemotherapy/autoimmune):** Folate antagonist; methylfolate may reduce efficacy (consult oncologist)
- **Anticonvulsants (Phenytoin, Valproate):** Reduce folate levels; may need higher doses
- **Sulfasalazine (IBD medication):** Impairs folate absorption
- **Trimethoprim (antibiotic):** Folate antagonist; separate by 6+ hours
- **SSRIs/Antidepressants:** Methylfolate may enhance efficacy (beneficial interaction)

### Genetic Considerations (MTHFR Gene)
- **MTHFR C677T (TT genotype):** 70% reduced enzyme activity → REQUIRES methylfolate (not folic acid)
- **MTHFR A1298C (CC genotype):** Moderate reduction in enzyme activity
- **Compound heterozygote (CT + AC):** Significant impairment, methylfolate strongly recommended

## Vitamin B12 (Methylcobalamin)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Active Forms:** Methylcobalamin (methyl-B12), Adenosylcobalamin (mitochondrial form)
- **Inactive Form:** Cyanocobalamin (requires conversion)
- **Sublingual/Injectable:** Hydroxocobalamin (converts to active forms)

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:** 2.0 μg/day (adults)
- **Pregnant women:** 2.6 μg/day
- **Lactating women:** 2.8 μg/day
- **Vegans/vegetarians:** May require 5-10 μg/day due to lack of animal sources

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General population:** 500-1,000 μg/day (methylcobalamin)
- **B12 deficiency correction:** 1,000-2,000 μg/day (sublingual or oral)
- **Older adults (>65 years):** 1,000 μg/day (reduced intrinsic factor)
- **Target serum level:** >300 pmol/L (>400 pg/mL)
- **Clinical evidence:** High-dose methylcobalamin (500-1,000 μg) improves nerve function and energy metabolism

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Not established (no known toxicity)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established (water-soluble, excess excreted)
- **Safety profile:** Doses up to 5,000 μg/day studied without adverse effects

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** Morning, sublingual or with food
- **Rationale:** Water-soluble; morning supports energy production and methylation; sublingual bypasses intrinsic factor requirement

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Methylfolate (5-MTHF):** 400-800 μg - synergistic in methylation cycle
- **Vitamin B6 (P5P):** 10-25 mg - works together in homocysteine metabolism
- **Magnesium:** 200-400 mg - required for B12-dependent enzymes
- **Potassium:** May need monitoring during high-dose B12 repletion (rare hypokalemia risk)

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **High-dose Vitamin C:** >1,000 mg may degrade B12 in stomach (separate by 2 hours)
- **Alcohol:** Impairs B12 absorption

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **Metformin (diabetes):** Reduces B12 absorption by 30% (supplement required)
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors (Omeprazole, Esomeprazole):** Reduce stomach acid → impaired B12 absorption
- **H2 Blockers (Ranitidine):** Similar to PPIs, reduce B12 absorption
- **Colchicine (gout medication):** Impairs B12 absorption
- **Nitrous oxide (anesthesia):** Inactivates B12; avoid supplementation 24h before surgery

### Genetic Considerations
- **FUT2 gene (non-secretor status):** Impaired intrinsic factor → use sublingual or high-dose oral B12
- **TCN2 gene polymorphisms:** May affect B12 transport; higher doses may be needed

## Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA/DHA)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Active Forms:**
  - EPA (Eicosapentaenoic Acid) - anti-inflammatory
  - DHA (Docosahexaenoic Acid) - brain/eye health
- **Sources:** Fish oil (triglyceride or ethyl ester), krill oil (phospholipid), algae oil (vegan)
- **Preferred form:** Re-esterified triglyceride (rTG) - best absorption

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:** 250 mg combined EPA+DHA
- **Pregnant/lactating women:** 450 mg EPA+DHA (including 200 mg DHA)
- **Cardiovascular disease prevention:** 1,000 mg EPA+DHA (EFSA recommendation)

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General health:** 1,000-2,000 mg EPA+DHA (combined)
- **Cardiovascular disease:** 2,000-4,000 mg EPA+DHA
- **Cognitive health:** 1,000+ mg DHA (higher doses 3+ g/day more pronounced)
- **Depression/mood:** 1,000-2,000 mg EPA (EPA-dominant formulas)
- **Inflammation:** 2,000-3,000 mg EPA
- **Clinical evidence:**
  - [2024-2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12628397/): 1-4 g/day cardiovascular benefits
  - [Cognitive study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-16129-8): 1+ g/day cognitive benefits, 3+ g/day more pronounced

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** >5,000 mg/kg (animal models, extremely safe)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 5,000 mg/day (EFSA)
- **Toxicity concerns:**
  - Increased bleeding risk at >3,000 mg/day (especially with anticoagulants)
  - Immune suppression at very high doses (>10,000 mg/day)
- **Safe chronic dose:** Up to 5,000 mg/day studied long-term

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** With largest meal (lunch or dinner)
- **Rationale:** Fat-soluble, requires dietary fat for absorption; better tolerance with food (reduces fishy aftertaste/burps)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin E (d-alpha tocopherol):** 10-15 IU per 1,000 mg Omega-3 - prevents oxidation
- **Astaxanthin:** 4-8 mg - potent antioxidant, enhances Omega-3 stability
- **Vitamin D3:** Synergistic for cardiovascular and immune health
- **Phospholipids:** Enhance absorption (found naturally in krill oil)

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **High-dose Omega-6 (linoleic acid):** Competes for enzymes; keep Omega-6:Omega-3 ratio <4:1
- **Trans fats:** Antagonize Omega-3 metabolism

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **Anticoagulants (Warfarin, Heparin):** Increased bleeding risk at >3,000 mg/day (monitor INR)
- **Antiplatelet drugs (Aspirin, Clopidogrel):** Additive antiplatelet effect (caution at >2,000 mg/day)
- **Blood pressure medications:** May enhance hypotensive effect (beneficial but monitor)
- **Orlistat:** Reduces fat-soluble nutrient absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **Cyclosporine (immunosuppressant):** Omega-3 may enhance effect

### Genetic Considerations
- **APOE4 allele:** Higher doses (2,000-3,000 mg DHA) may be more beneficial for cognitive protection
- **FADS1/FADS2 genes:** Polymorphisms affect EPA/DHA synthesis from ALA; direct EPA/DHA supplementation recommended

## Magnesium (Glycinate)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Preferred Form:** Magnesium Glycinate (Magnesium bis-glycinate chelate)
- **Other Forms:**
  - Magnesium citrate (laxative effect)
  - Magnesium oxide (low bioavailability ~4%)
  - Magnesium threonate (brain-specific)
  - Magnesium taurate (cardiovascular)
- **Elemental Magnesium:** Glycinate is ~14% elemental Mg by weight

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:**
  - Men: 350 mg/day
  - Women: 300 mg/day
  - Pregnant women: 310 mg/day

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General health:** 300-500 mg elemental magnesium
- **Sleep improvement:** 250-500 mg elemental (glycinate form)
- **Anxiety/stress:** 300-600 mg elemental
- **Migraine prevention:** 400-600 mg elemental
- **Muscle cramps/recovery:** 400-500 mg elemental
- **Clinical evidence:**
  - [2024 RCT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12412596/): 250mg elemental + 1523mg glycine improved sleep quality
  - [Systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11136869/): Magnesium reduces anxiety symptoms

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50 (oral):** >5,000 mg/kg (animal models, very low toxicity)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 350 mg/day from supplements (beyond diet)
- **Toxicity symptoms:** Diarrhea (>500 mg elemental in single dose), hypermagnesemia (rare, kidney dysfunction)
- **Note:** Glycinate form better tolerated than oxide/citrate (less laxative effect)

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** Evening, 30-60 minutes before bed
- **Rationale:**
  - Promotes relaxation and sleep (GABA agonist, NMDA antagonist)
  - Glycine component also promotes sleep
  - Better absorbed on empty stomach, but can take with light snack if needed

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin B6 (P5P):** 10-25 mg - enhances magnesium cellular uptake
- **Vitamin D3:** 1,000-2,000 IU - requires magnesium for activation
- **Taurine:** 500-1,000 mg - synergistic for cardiovascular and sleep benefits
- **Potassium:** Maintain balance (avoid isolated high-dose magnesium without adequate potassium)

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Calcium:** >500 mg competes for absorption (separate by 2+ hours)
- **Zinc:** >30 mg competes for absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **Iron:** >25 mg competes for absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **High-fiber foods:** May reduce absorption (take 1 hour away from large meals)
- **Phytates (grains, legumes):** Bind magnesium, reducing absorption

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **Bisphosphonates (Alendronate):** Reduces absorption; separate by 2+ hours
- **Antibiotics (Tetracyclines, Fluoroquinolones):** Form complexes, reducing efficacy; separate by 2-4 hours
- **Diuretics (Furosemide):** Increase magnesium loss (may need higher doses)
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors:** Long-term use reduces magnesium absorption
- **Muscle relaxants:** Magnesium may enhance effect (caution)

### Genetic Considerations
- **COMT Val158Met (Val/Val):** Higher magnesium needs due to faster dopamine metabolism
- **TRPM6/TRPM7 genes:** Polymorphisms affect magnesium homeostasis; may require higher doses

## Zinc (Picolinate)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Preferred Form:** Zinc Picolinate (highest bioavailability ~20-25%)
- **Other Forms:**
  - Zinc gluconate (moderate bioavailability)
  - Zinc citrate (good bioavailability)
  - Zinc oxide (low bioavailability ~5%)
  - Zinc monomethionine (OptiZinc®)

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:**
  - Men: 10 mg/day
  - Women: 7 mg/day
  - Pregnant women: 10 mg/day
  - Lactating women: 11 mg/day

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General health:** 15-30 mg elemental zinc
- **Immune support:** 15-25 mg (chronic), 75-100 mg short-term (<2 weeks for acute illness)
- **Skin health (acne):** 30-40 mg
- **Fertility (men):** 25-50 mg
- **Vegan/vegetarian:** 20-30 mg (phytates reduce absorption)

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** ~350 mg/kg (animal models)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 25 mg/day (EU), 40 mg/day (US)
- **Toxicity symptoms:**
  - Acute: >150 mg single dose - nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain
  - Chronic: >50 mg/day long-term - copper deficiency, immune suppression, HDL reduction
- **Safe upper range:** 40 mg/day for short-term (<8 weeks), 25 mg/day long-term

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** Evening, 1-2 hours before bed, away from food
- **Alternative:** Morning on empty stomach if evening causes nausea
- **Rationale:** Absorption best on empty stomach; taking with food reduces bioavailability by 50%

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Copper:** 1-2 mg copper for every 15 mg zinc (prevent copper deficiency)
- **Vitamin B6:** Enhances zinc utilization
- **Vitamin A:** Synergistic for immune function and skin health
- **Small amount of protein:** Can enhance absorption if taken with food

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Calcium:** >500 mg reduces zinc absorption by 50% (separate by 2+ hours)
- **Iron:** >25 mg competes for absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **Magnesium:** >300 mg competes for absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **Phytates (grains, legumes):** Reduce zinc absorption by 50% (take away from high-fiber meals)
- **Coffee/tea (tannins):** Reduce absorption (separate by 1 hour)

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **Antibiotics (Tetracyclines, Fluoroquinolones):** Form complexes, reducing efficacy; separate by 2-4 hours
- **Penicillamine (Wilson's disease):** Zinc reduces drug absorption; separate by 2 hours
- **Thiazide diuretics:** Increase zinc loss; may need higher doses
- **ACE inhibitors (blood pressure):** May reduce zinc levels
- **Cisplatin (chemotherapy):** Zinc may protect against side effects (beneficial interaction)

### Genetic Considerations
- **ZIP4/SLC39A4 gene:** Mutations affect zinc absorption (rare acrodermatitis enteropathica)
- **Metallothionein genes (MT1A, MT2A):** Affect zinc storage and utilization

## Selenium (Selenomethionine)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Preferred Form:** Selenomethionine (organic, best absorbed ~90%)
- **Other Forms:**
  - Sodium selenite (inorganic, ~50% absorption)
  - Selenocysteine (organic)
  - Selenium yeast (mixture of organic forms)

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:** 55 μg/day (adults)
- **Pregnant women:** 60 μg/day
- **Lactating women:** 70 μg/day

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General health:** 100-200 μg/day
- **Thyroid health:** 100-200 μg/day (supports iodothyronine deiodinases)
- **Immune support:** 100-200 μg/day
- **Antioxidant protection:** 200 μg/day
- **Cancer prevention (controversial):** 200 μg/day (some studies show benefit, others neutral)

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** ~5-7 mg/kg (narrow therapeutic window)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 300 μg/day
- **Toxicity symptoms (selenosis):**
  - >400 μg/day chronic: Hair loss, brittle nails, garlic breath, nausea, fatigue
  - >1,000 μg/day: Severe neurological effects
- **Safe upper range:** 200 μg/day long-term, 300 μg/day short-term

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** Morning with breakfast
- **Rationale:** Can be taken with or without food; morning for consistency

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin E:** Synergistic antioxidant effect
- **Iodine:** Works together for thyroid hormone metabolism (but see antagonist section)
- **Vitamin C:** Enhances selenium antioxidant activity
- **Zinc:** Synergistic for immune function

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **High-dose Vitamin C:** >1,000 mg may reduce selenium absorption from selenite form (not selenomethionine)
- **Heavy metals (Mercury, Cadmium):** Selenium binds and detoxifies, but depletes selenium stores

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **Anticoagulants (Warfarin):** High-dose selenium may enhance effect (caution >200 μg/day)
- **Chemotherapy (Cisplatin):** Selenium may reduce efficacy (avoid during treatment)
- **Statins:** Selenium may reduce cholesterol-lowering effect at very high doses (>400 μg)
- **Niacin:** Combined with selenium may reduce HDL cholesterol
- **Barbiturates:** May increase selenium metabolism

### Genetic Considerations
- **SELENOP gene:** Polymorphisms affect selenium transport; higher doses may be needed
- **GPX1 gene (Glutathione Peroxidase 1):** Variants may benefit from higher selenium (200 μg/day)

## Vitamin K2 (MK-7)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Active Forms:**
  - MK-7 (Menaquinone-7) - long half-life (72 hours), preferred form
  - MK-4 (Menaquinone-4) - short half-life (1 hour), requires multiple daily doses
- **Vitamin K1 (Phylloquinone):** Plant-based, primarily for blood clotting

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:** 75 μg/day (total Vitamin K, mostly K1)
- **No specific RDI for K2 established**

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **Bone health:** 100-200 μg MK-7/day
- **Cardiovascular health:** 100-200 μg MK-7/day (reduces arterial calcification)
- **With Vitamin D supplementation:** 100-200 μg MK-7 (directs calcium to bones)
- **Clinical evidence:** 180 μg MK-7/day improves bone density and reduces arterial stiffness

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Not established (very low toxicity)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **Toxicity concerns:** No known toxicity from MK-7 supplementation
- **Safe upper range:** Up to 1,000 μg/day studied without adverse effects

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** Morning or evening with Vitamin D (fat-containing meal)
- **Rationale:** Fat-soluble, requires dietary fat for absorption

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin D3:** REQUIRED - works synergistically for bone and cardiovascular health
- **Calcium:** K2 directs calcium to bones (not arteries)
- **Magnesium:** Supports bone mineralization
- **Dietary fat:** 10-15g fat with meal (increases absorption)

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Vitamin A (retinol):** Very high doses (>10,000 IU) may interfere with K2 function
- **Vitamin E (high dose):** >400 IU may reduce K2 absorption

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **⚠️ WARFARIN (Coumadin) - ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATION:**
  - Vitamin K2 directly antagonizes warfarin
  - DO NOT supplement K2 if on warfarin without physician approval
  - May require warfarin dose adjustment and INR monitoring
- **Other anticoagulants (Heparin, DOAC):** Less interaction than warfarin, but caution advised
- **Antibiotics (broad-spectrum):** Reduce gut K2 production; may need supplementation

### Genetic Considerations
- **VKORC1 gene:** Polymorphisms affect Vitamin K metabolism and warfarin sensitivity
- **GGCX gene:** Affects activation of K-dependent proteins (osteocalcin, MGP)

## Iron (Bisglycinate)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Preferred Form:** Iron Bisglycinate (Ferrochel®) - chelated, non-constipating, 25% absorption
- **Other Forms:**
  - Ferrous sulfate (20% absorption, constipating)
  - Ferrous gluconate (15% absorption)
  - Ferrous fumarate (33% elemental iron)
  - Heme iron polypeptide (from animal sources, best absorption)

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:**
  - Men: 10 mg/day
  - Women (premenopausal): 15-18 mg/day
  - Pregnant women: 27 mg/day
  - Lactating women: 10 mg/day
  - Postmenopausal women: 10 mg/day

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General health (with adequate intake):** 10-18 mg
- **Iron deficiency anemia:** 50-100 mg elemental iron (divided doses)
- **Pregnancy:** 27-60 mg elemental iron
- **Athletes (endurance):** 18-25 mg
- **Vegans/vegetarians:** 1.8× RDI (18-32 mg) due to non-heme iron sources

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** ~200-300 mg/kg elemental iron (highly toxic in overdose)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 45 mg/day elemental iron
- **Acute toxicity:** >20 mg/kg can be fatal (especially in children)
- **Chronic toxicity:** >50 mg/day long-term - hemochromatosis, organ damage
- **⚠️ KEEP AWAY FROM CHILDREN - leading cause of poisoning deaths in children**

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** Morning on empty stomach (1 hour before food)
- **Alternative:** Evening if morning causes nausea (absorption reduced by 50% with food)
- **Rationale:** Best absorption on empty stomach, but bisglycinate form tolerated better with food than other forms

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin C:** 100-200 mg - increases non-heme iron absorption by 3-4×
- **Vitamin B12 & Folate:** Support red blood cell production
- **Copper:** 1-2 mg - required for iron incorporation into hemoglobin
- **Vitamin A:** Mobilizes iron from storage

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Calcium:** >300 mg reduces iron absorption by 50% (separate by 2+ hours)
- **Zinc:** >25 mg competes for absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **Magnesium:** >200 mg reduces absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **Coffee/tea (tannins):** Reduce absorption by 60% (separate by 1-2 hours)
- **Phytates (grains, legumes):** Reduce absorption by 50% (take away from meals)
- **Eggs (phosphoprotein):** Reduce absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **High-fiber foods:** Reduce absorption

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **Levothyroxine (thyroid hormone):** Separate by 4 hours (iron reduces absorption)
- **Levodopa (Parkinson's):** Iron reduces absorption; separate by 2 hours
- **Antibiotics (Tetracyclines, Fluoroquinolones):** Form complexes; separate by 2-4 hours
- **Bisphosphonates (osteoporosis):** Separate by 2 hours
- **PPIs (Omeprazole):** Reduce iron absorption (need higher doses or bisglycinate form)
- **H2 blockers (Ranitidine):** Reduce iron absorption

### Genetic Considerations
- **HFE gene (C282Y, H63D mutations):** Hemochromatosis risk - DO NOT supplement iron without testing
- **TF/TFRC genes:** Affect iron transport and storage
- **TMPRSS6 gene:** Mutations cause iron-refractory iron deficiency anemia

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ TEST BEFORE SUPPLEMENTING:** Check ferritin, serum iron, TIBC before iron supplementation
- **⚠️ HEMOCHROMATOSIS RISK:** 1 in 200-300 people have genetic iron overload; supplementation dangerous
- **⚠️ MEN & POSTMENOPAUSAL WOMEN:** Rarely need iron supplementation; test first

## Calcium (Citrate)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Preferred Form:** Calcium Citrate (21% elemental calcium, absorbed without food)
- **Other Forms:**
  - Calcium carbonate (40% elemental, requires stomach acid)
  - Calcium lactate (13% elemental)
  - Calcium gluconate (9% elemental)
  - Microcrystalline hydroxyapatite (MCHA) - from bone, includes other minerals

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:**
  - Adults (18-24 years): 1,000 mg/day
  - Adults (>24 years): 800 mg/day
  - Pregnant/lactating women: 1,000-1,200 mg/day
  - Postmenopausal women: 1,200 mg/day

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General health:** 500-800 mg supplemental (beyond dietary intake)
- **Bone health:** 1,000-1,200 mg total (diet + supplement)
- **Osteoporosis prevention:** 1,200 mg total + Vitamin D + K2
- **Note:** Most people get 300-700 mg from diet; supplement the difference

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** >5,000 mg/kg (very low acute toxicity)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 2,500 mg/day total
- **Toxicity symptoms:**
  - Hypercalcemia: >2,500 mg/day long-term - nausea, vomiting, kidney stones
  - Vascular calcification risk: >1,500 mg supplemental without K2
- **Safe upper range:** 1,200 mg/day total (diet + supplement)

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** Divided doses (2-3× daily), with or without food
- **Rationale:**
  - Maximum single-dose absorption: 500 mg (excess not absorbed)
  - Citrate form absorbed without food (unlike carbonate)
  - Evening dose may support bone remodeling (occurs at night)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **⚠️ VITAMIN D3 (REQUIRED):** 1,000-2,000 IU - needed for calcium absorption (increases from 10% → 40%)
- **⚠️ VITAMIN K2 (REQUIRED):** 100-200 μg - directs calcium to bones (prevents arterial calcification)
- **Magnesium:** 200-400 mg - balance (ideal Ca:Mg ratio 2:1)
- **Vitamin C:** Supports collagen production for bone matrix
- **Boron:** 3 mg - reduces calcium excretion

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Iron:** >18 mg reduces absorption by 50% (separate by 2+ hours)
- **Zinc:** >15 mg reduces absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **Magnesium:** >300 mg competes for absorption (separate by 2 hours or balance ratio)
- **High-fiber foods:** Phytates reduce absorption
- **High-sodium foods:** Increase calcium excretion
- **Caffeine:** Increases calcium excretion (limit to <300 mg/day)
- **Phosphorus (sodas):** Excess disrupts calcium balance

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **Bisphosphonates (Alendronate):** Separate by 2+ hours (calcium reduces absorption)
- **Levothyroxine (thyroid):** Separate by 4 hours (calcium reduces absorption by 30%)
- **Antibiotics (Tetracyclines, Fluoroquinolones):** Form complexes; separate by 2-4 hours
- **Thiazide diuretics:** Reduce calcium excretion (risk of hypercalcemia; may need lower calcium dose)
- **Corticosteroids:** Increase calcium excretion (may need higher doses)
- **Antacids (calcium-based):** Avoid exceeding UL when combined with supplements

### Genetic Considerations
- **VDR gene (Bsm1, Fok1):** Polymorphisms affect calcium absorption efficiency; may need higher Vitamin D
- **CASR gene (Calcium-Sensing Receptor):** Rare mutations affect calcium homeostasis

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ CARDIOVASCULAR RISK:** High-dose calcium supplements (>1,000 mg) WITHOUT Vitamin K2 may increase arterial calcification and CV risk
- **⚠️ KIDNEY STONES:** History of calcium oxalate stones - use citrate form, avoid >1,200 mg/day
- **⚠️ HYPERCALCEMIA CONDITIONS:** Hyperparathyroidism, sarcoidosis, cancer - avoid supplementation

## Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Standard Form:** L-Ascorbic Acid (water-soluble)
- **Buffered Forms:**

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)

  - Women: 75 mg/day
  - Smokers: +35 mg/day
  - Pregnant women: 85 mg/day
  - Lactating women: 120 mg/day

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General health:** 200-500 mg/day (divided doses)
- **Immune support:** 500-1,000 mg/day (maintenance), 1,000-2,000 mg/day (acute illness)
- **Antioxidant protection:** 500-1,000 mg/day
- **Collagen synthesis:** 500 mg/day
- **Iron absorption enhancement:** 100-200 mg with iron supplement
- **Note:** >200 mg single dose - absorption decreases; excess excreted

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** >11,000 mg/kg (extremely safe)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 2,000 mg/day
- **Toxicity symptoms:**
  - Diarrhea: >2,000 mg/day (osmotic effect)
  - Kidney stones (oxalate): >2,000 mg/day long-term in susceptible individuals
  - Gastric upset: High doses on empty stomach
- **Safe upper range:** 1,000-2,000 mg/day (divided doses)

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Optimal:** Divided doses (2-3× daily) with meals
- **Rationale:**
  - Water-soluble, 2-3 hour half-life (frequent dosing maintains levels)
  - With meals reduces gastric upset
  - Morning + afternoon for energy support

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Bioflavonoids (Quercetin, Rutin):** Enhance Vitamin C recycling and activity
- **Vitamin E:** Synergistic antioxidant (C regenerates E)
- **Iron (non-heme):** Vitamin C increases absorption 3-4× (beneficial interaction)
- **Selenium:** Synergistic antioxidant effect
- **Collagen peptides:** C supports collagen synthesis

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Copper:** High-dose Vitamin C (>1,500 mg) may reduce copper absorption
- **Vitamin B12:** Very high doses (>1,000 mg) may degrade B12 in stomach (separate by 2 hours)

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)
- **Aspirin/NSAIDs:** High-dose Vitamin C may increase gastric irritation
- **Warfarin:** Very high doses (>1,000 mg) may reduce anticoagulant effect (controversial)
- **Chemotherapy (some types):** Antioxidants may reduce efficacy (avoid during treatment)
- **Statins & Niacin:** High-dose Vitamin C may reduce HDL cholesterol benefits (controversial)
- **Estrogen (oral contraceptives, HRT):** Vitamin C may increase estrogen levels
- **Acetaminophen (Paracetamol):** Vitamin C may increase acetaminophen levels

### Genetic Considerations
- **GSTM1/GSTT1 null genotypes:** Higher oxidative stress; may benefit from higher Vitamin C (500-1,000 mg)
- **SLC23A1 gene (Vitamin C transporter):** Polymorphisms affect tissue distribution

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ KIDNEY STONES:** History of calcium oxalate stones - limit to 500 mg/day
- **⚠️ HEMOCHROMATOSIS:** High-dose Vitamin C increases iron absorption (avoid >200 mg)
- **⚠️ G6PD DEFICIENCY:** Very high doses (>1,000 mg) may cause hemolytic anemia (rare)

### Scientific Name & Forms

  - Retinol (animal sources)

- **Beta-Carotene LD50:** Non-toxic (no UL established, excess excreted or stored as carotenoids)

  - Acute: >100,000 IU - nausea, headache, blurred vision
  - Chronic: >10,000 IU/day long-term - liver damage, bone loss, birth defects

- **Vitamin E:** Protects Vitamin A from oxidation
- **Dietary fat:** 10-15g fat with meal (increases absorption by 50%)
- **Vitamin D:** Works synergistically (but see antagonist section)

- **High-dose Vitamin D:** >5,000 IU may compete for receptor binding (both use nuclear receptors)
- **High-dose Vitamin E:** >800 IU may reduce Vitamin A absorption
- **Alcohol:** Synergistic liver toxicity with preformed Vitamin A (avoid)

### Drug Interactions (Medical Contraindications)

- **⚠️ RETINOIDS (Tretinoin, Adapalene):** Additive toxicity risk; avoid preformed Vitamin A
- **Orlistat (weight loss):** Reduces fat-soluble vitamin absorption; may need higher doses
- **Cholestyramine (cholesterol):** Reduces Vitamin A absorption
- **Hepatotoxic drugs:** Alcohol, Methotrexate - additive liver toxicity with preformed Vitamin A

### Genetic Considerations
- **BCO1 gene (Beta-Carotene Oxygenase 1):** Polymorphisms reduce beta-carotene → retinol conversion (45-69% reduced activity)
  - Carriers may need preformed Vitamin A (low-dose 3,000-5,000 IU retinol)
- **BCMO1 variants:** Common in European populations (~40% have reduced conversion)

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ PREGNANCY:** NEVER exceed 5,000 IU preformed Vitamin A (teratogenic); use beta-carotene instead
- **⚠️ SMOKERS:** High-dose beta-carotene (>20 mg/day) increases lung cancer risk (AVOID)
- **⚠️ LIVER DISEASE:** Preformed Vitamin A accumulates in liver; avoid supplementation
- **⚠️ BCO1 GENETIC VARIANT:** If poor converter, use low-dose preformed Vitamin A (not beta-carotene)

## Magnesium (7 Forms)

### Scientific Name & Forms

**1. Magnesium Glycinate (Bisglycinate)**
- **Bioavailability:** Excellent (~20-30%)
- **Best for:** Sleep, relaxation, anxiety, muscle recovery
- **Dose:** 200-400 mg elemental
- **Pros:** Non-laxative, calming (glycine is inhibitory neurotransmitter)
- **Cons:** Slightly more expensive

**2. Magnesium Citrate**
- **Bioavailability:** Good (~16%)
- **Best for:** Constipation, bowel regularity
- **Dose:** 200-400 mg elemental
- **Pros:** Affordable, effective laxative
- **Cons:** Can cause loose stools (dose-dependent)

**3. Magnesium L-Threonate (Magtein®)**
- **Bioavailability:** Brain-specific (crosses blood-brain barrier)
- **Best for:** Cognitive function, memory, brain health
- **Dose:** 1,500-2,000 mg (144 mg elemental magnesium)
- **Pros:** Only form proven to increase brain magnesium levels
- **Cons:** Expensive, lower elemental magnesium per dose

**4. Magnesium Taurate**
- **Bioavailability:** Good
- **Best for:** Cardiovascular health, blood pressure, heart rhythm
- **Dose:** 200-400 mg elemental
- **Pros:** Taurine + magnesium synergy for heart health
- **Cons:** Less common, higher cost

**5. Magnesium Malate**
- **Bioavailability:** Good
- **Best for:** Energy, chronic fatigue, fibromyalgia, muscle pain
- **Dose:** 300-600 mg elemental
- **Pros:** Malic acid supports ATP production (energy)
- **Cons:** Should be taken morning (energizing)

**6. Magnesium Oxide**
- **Bioavailability:** Poor (~4%)
- **Best for:** Budget option, laxative
- **Dose:** 400-800 mg (most excreted, not absorbed)
- **Pros:** Cheap, high elemental magnesium per capsule
- **Cons:** Low absorption, mainly laxative effect

**7. Magnesium Chloride (Topical)**
- **Bioavailability:** Transdermal absorption (bypasses GI)
- **Best for:** Muscle soreness, cramps, topical application
- **Form:** Magnesium oil spray, bath flakes
- **Pros:** No GI side effects, localized muscle relief
- **Cons:** Can sting on broken skin

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **EU/Nordic RDI:**
  - Men: 350 mg/day
  - Women: 300 mg/day
  - Pregnant women: 310 mg/day

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General health:** 300-500 mg elemental magnesium
- **Sleep (glycinate):** 250-500 mg evening
- **Cognitive (threonate):** 1,500-2,000 mg (144 mg elemental)
- **Cardiovascular (taurate):** 300-500 mg
- **Energy/fatigue (malate):** 300-600 mg morning
- **Constipation (citrate):** 200-400 mg (adjust to bowel tolerance)
- **Athletes:** 400-600 mg (increased losses through sweat)

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50 (oral):** >5,000 mg/kg
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** 350 mg/day from supplements (beyond diet)
- **Toxicity symptoms:** Diarrhea (>500 mg single dose), hypermagnesemia (rare, kidney dysfunction)

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Glycinate:** Evening, 30-60 min before bed (sleep, relaxation)
- **Threonate:** Morning or afternoon (cognitive support)
- **Malate:** Morning (energy, can interfere with sleep)
- **Taurate:** Morning or evening (cardiovascular)
- **Citrate:** Evening or as needed (constipation)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin B6 (P5P):** Enhances magnesium cellular uptake
- **Vitamin D3:** Requires magnesium for activation (25-hydroxylation, 1,25-dihydroxylation)
- **Taurine:** Synergistic for cardiovascular and sleep (especially with taurate form)
- **Potassium:** Maintain balance (avoid isolated high-dose magnesium without adequate potassium)

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Calcium:** >500 mg competes for absorption (separate by 2+ hours)
- **Zinc:** >30 mg competes for absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **Iron:** >25 mg competes for absorption (separate by 2 hours)
- **High-fiber foods:** May reduce absorption (take 1 hour away from large meals)
- **Phytates (grains, legumes):** Bind magnesium, reducing absorption

### Drug Interactions
- **Bisphosphonates (Alendronate):** Reduces absorption; separate by 2+ hours
- **Antibiotics (Tetracyclines, Fluoroquinolones):** Form complexes, reducing efficacy; separate by 2-4 hours
- **Diuretics (Furosemide):** Increase magnesium loss (may need higher doses)
- **Proton Pump Inhibitors:** Long-term use reduces magnesium absorption
- **Muscle relaxants:** Magnesium may enhance effect (caution)

### Genetic Considerations
- **COMT Val158Met (Val/Val):** Higher magnesium needs due to faster dopamine metabolism
- **TRPM6/TRPM7 genes:** Polymorphisms affect magnesium homeostasis; may require higher doses

### Form Selection Guide
| Condition | Recommended Form | Dose |
|-----------|------------------|------|
| Sleep, anxiety | Glycinate | 250-500 mg evening |
| Cognitive function | Threonate | 1,500-2,000 mg |
| Cardiovascular | Taurate | 300-500 mg |
| Energy, fatigue | Malate | 300-600 mg morning |
| Constipation | Citrate | 200-400 mg (adjust) |
| Budget option | Oxide | 400-800 mg (low absorption) |
| Muscle soreness (topical) | Chloride oil | Apply topically |
