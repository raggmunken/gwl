## L-Theanine

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Chemical Name:** γ-glutamylethylamide, N5-ethyl-L-glutamine
- **Source:** Green tea (Camellia sinensis)
- **Synthetic:** Available as pure L-theanine (Suntheanine®)

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Food source provides 8-50 mg/cup green tea

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General relaxation:** 100-200 mg
- **Stress/anxiety:** 200-400 mg/day
- **Sleep support (with magnesium):** 200-400 mg before bed
- **Focus (with caffeine):** 100-200 mg L-theanine + 50-100 mg caffeine (2:1 ratio)
- **Note:** Crosses blood-brain barrier, effects within 30-60 min

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** >5,000 mg/kg (very safe)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** Up to 900 mg/day studied safely

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Daytime (focus):** With coffee/caffeine (L-theanine smooths out jitters)
- **Evening (sleep):** 30-60 min before bed
- **As needed:** Anxiety, stress (works within 30-60 min)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Caffeine:** Synergistic for focus without jitters (2:1 theanine:caffeine ratio)
- **Magnesium:** Synergistic for relaxation, sleep
- **GABA:** May enhance calming effects

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Stimulants (high-dose):** May blunt stimulant effects (can be desired)

### Drug Interactions
- **Blood pressure medications:** L-theanine may lower blood pressure; monitor
- **Sedatives:** May enhance sedative effects (beneficial for sleep)

### Genetic Considerations
- **COMT Val158Met (Met/Met - "worrier"):** May benefit more from L-theanine for anxiety

## L-Tyrosine & N-Acetyl L-Tyrosine (NALT)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **L-Tyrosine:** Standard form (requires more acidic stomach)
- **N-Acetyl L-Tyrosine (NALT):** More water-soluble, better absorption
- **Precursor to:** Dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine, thyroid hormones

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Non-essential amino acid (body synthesizes from phenylalanine)

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **Cognitive support (stress, focus):** 500-2,000 mg L-tyrosine or 300-600 mg NALT
- **Depression (adjunct):** 1,000-2,500 mg L-tyrosine
- **Thyroid support:** 500-1,000 mg
- **Athletic performance:** 100-150 mg/kg bodyweight (taken 30-60 min pre-workout)

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Very high (low toxicity)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** >2,000 mg may cause jitteriness, anxiety in sensitive individuals

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Morning:** Empty stomach (competes with other amino acids for absorption)
- **Pre-workout:** 30-60 min before exercise
- **Avoid evening:** May interfere with sleep (stimulating)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin B6, Folate, Copper:** Cofactors for dopamine synthesis
- **Vitamin C, Iron:** Support catecholamine synthesis
- **Rhodiola, Ashwagandha:** Synergistic for stress adaptation

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Other large neutral amino acids:** Compete for absorption (take away from protein-rich meals)
- **5-HTP/Tryptophan:** May compete for transport into brain

### Drug Interactions
- **⚠️ MAO INHIBITORS:** Tyrosine may increase tyramine levels → hypertensive crisis; AVOID
- **Levodopa (Parkinson's):** Tyrosine may reduce efficacy; separate by 2 hours
- **Thyroid medications:** Tyrosine is precursor to thyroid hormones; monitor thyroid function

### Genetic Considerations
- **COMT Val158Met (Val/Val - "warrior"):** Faster dopamine breakdown, may benefit from tyrosine
- **TH gene (Tyrosine Hydroxylase):** Variants may affect dopamine synthesis capacity

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ HYPERTHYROIDISM:** May worsen condition (tyrosine is thyroid hormone precursor)
- **⚠️ MELANOMA RISK:** Theoretical concern (tyrosine is melanin precursor); avoid if melanoma history

## L-Tryptophan & 5-HTP

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **L-Tryptophan:** Essential amino acid, converts to 5-HTP → serotonin → melatonin
- **5-HTP (5-Hydroxytryptophan):** Intermediate metabolite, one step closer to serotonin
- **Source:** L-tryptophan from food/supplements, 5-HTP from Griffonia simplicifolia seeds

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **L-Tryptophan RDI:** ~4 mg/kg bodyweight (~280 mg for 70kg person)
- **5-HTP:** No official RDI

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **L-Tryptophan:**
  - Sleep support: 500-1,500 mg
  - Mood support: 1,000-2,000 mg/day
  - Depression: 2,000-4,000 mg/day (clinical doses)
- **5-HTP:**
  - Sleep support: 50-150 mg before bed
  - Mood support: 50-300 mg/day (divided doses)
  - Depression: 100-300 mg/day

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Very high
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **Serotonin syndrome risk:** >300 mg 5-HTP (especially with SSRIs)

### When to Take (Timing)
- **L-Tryptophan:** Evening, empty stomach (30-60 min before bed)
- **5-HTP:** Evening for sleep, or divided doses for mood

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin B6, Magnesium:** Cofactors for serotonin synthesis
- **Vitamin C:** Supports conversion
- **Carbohydrates:** Enhance tryptophan transport into brain (insulin response)

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Other amino acids:** Compete for absorption (take away from meals)
- **Protein-rich meals:** Large neutral amino acids compete with tryptophan for brain uptake

### Drug Interactions
- **⚠️ SSRIs/SNRIs (Antidepressants):** Risk of serotonin syndrome with 5-HTP; AVOID or use low doses under medical supervision
- **⚠️ MAO INHIBITORS:** Increased serotonin → serotonin syndrome risk; AVOID
- **Tramadol, Dextromethorphan:** Serotonergic drugs; risk of serotonin syndrome with 5-HTP
- **Carbidopa (Parkinson's drug):** May enhance 5-HTP effects (beneficial but monitor)

### Genetic Considerations
- **5-HTTLPR (SLC6A4 gene - serotonin transporter):** Short allele associated with lower serotonin; may benefit from supplementation
- **TPH1/TPH2 genes (Tryptophan Hydroxylase):** Variants affect serotonin synthesis capacity

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ SEROTONIN SYNDROME:** With SSRIs + 5-HTP - symptoms include agitation, confusion, rapid heart rate, dilated pupils, muscle rigidity
- **⚠️ 5-HTP vs L-Tryptophan:** 5-HTP bypasses rate-limiting step, higher serotonin syndrome risk; L-tryptophan safer for long-term

## L-Carnitine & Acetyl-L-Carnitine (ALCAR)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **L-Carnitine:** Transports fatty acids into mitochondria for energy (β-oxidation)
- **Acetyl-L-Carnitine (ALCAR):** Crosses blood-brain barrier, neuroprotective, cognitive support
- **Propionyl-L-Carnitine:** Cardiovascular-specific
- **Glycine Propionyl-L-Carnitine (GPLC):** Enhanced NO production, blood flow

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Body synthesizes from lysine + methionine

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **L-Carnitine:**
  - Fat loss, athletic performance: 1,000-3,000 mg/day
  - Cardiovascular health: 1,000-2,000 mg/day
- **Acetyl-L-Carnitine (ALCAR):**
  - Cognitive function, neuroprotection: 500-2,000 mg/day
  - Depression (elderly): 1,000-3,000 mg/day
  - Chronic fatigue: 1,000-2,000 mg/day

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** >10,000 mg/kg
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** >3,000 mg/day may cause fishy body odor (TMAO production), nausea

### When to Take (Timing)
- **L-Carnitine:** Morning or pre-workout (30-60 min before exercise)
- **ALCAR:** Morning or afternoon (can be stimulating)
- **Avoid evening:** May interfere with sleep

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Omega-3:** Synergistic for fat metabolism
- **Alpha-lipoic acid:** Synergistic for mitochondrial function, glucose metabolism
- **CoQ10:** Mitochondrial energy support

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Hypothyroidism medications:** Carnitine may reduce thyroid hormone uptake into cells (theoretical)

### Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** L-carnitine may enhance anticoagulant effect (monitor INR)
- **Thyroid medications:** May reduce efficacy (theoretical); monitor thyroid function

### Genetic Considerations
- **SLC22A5 gene (Carnitine transporter):** Deficiency causes primary carnitine deficiency

## L-Glutamine

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **L-Glutamine:** Most abundant amino acid in body, conditionally essential
- **Uses:** Gut health, immune function, muscle recovery, anti-catabolic

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Non-essential (body synthesizes)

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **Gut health (leaky gut, IBS):** 5-10 g/day (divided doses)
- **Immune support:** 5-10 g/day
- **Muscle recovery (athletes):** 10-20 g/day (post-workout)
- **Chemotherapy side effects:** 10 g 3×/day (medical supervision)

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Very high
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** Up to 40 g/day studied safely (short-term)

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Gut health:** Divided doses (morning, afternoon, evening) on empty stomach
- **Athletic recovery:** Post-workout + before bed

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Probiotics:** Synergistic for gut health
- **Zinc:** Supports gut barrier function
- **Vitamin C:** Immune synergy

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **None significant**

### Drug Interactions
- **Minimal:** No significant drug interactions

### Genetic Considerations
- **GLS/GLS2 genes (Glutaminase):** Affect glutamine metabolism

## Glycine

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Glycine:** Simplest amino acid, inhibitory neurotransmitter
- **Uses:** Sleep, collagen production, glutathione synthesis, neuroprotection

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Non-essential

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **Sleep support:** 3,000 mg (3g) before bed
- **Collagen synthesis:** 3-5 g/day
- **Metabolic health:** 5-15 g/day
- **Schizophrenia adjunct:** 15-60 g/day (medical supervision)

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Very high
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** Up to 90 g/day studied without severe adverse effects (mild GI upset)

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Sleep:** 3g, 1 hour before bed (lowers core body temperature, improves sleep quality)
- **Collagen:** With meals

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Magnesium:** Synergistic for sleep
- **Vitamin C, Proline, Lysine:** Collagen synthesis
- **NAC:** Glutathione synthesis

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **None significant**

### Drug Interactions
- **Clozapine (antipsychotic):** Glycine may enhance efficacy (beneficial)

### Genetic Considerations
- **GLYT1/SLC6A9 gene (Glycine transporter):** Variants affect glycine levels

## Creatine Monohydrate

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Creatine Monohydrate:** Most studied form, ~88% creatine + ~12% water
- **Other Forms:** Creatine HCl, Kre-Alkalyn (less studied, more expensive)
- **Function:** ATP regeneration (PCr → ATP), muscle energy, neuroprotection

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Body synthesizes ~1g/day from arginine + glycine

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **Maintenance:** 3-5 g/day (no loading needed)
- **Loading phase (optional):** 20 g/day × 5-7 days, then 3-5 g maintenance
- **Cognitive benefits:** 5 g/day
- **Vegetarians/vegans:** 5-10 g/day (no dietary creatine)

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Very high
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **Long-term safety:** 5 g/day for years studied without adverse effects
- **Kidney concerns:** No evidence of harm in healthy individuals; avoid if kidney disease

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Any time:** Creatine saturation is cumulative (timing less important than daily consistency)
- **Post-workout (with carbs):** May slightly enhance uptake (insulin-mediated)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Carbohydrates:** Enhance creatine uptake via insulin (20-50g simple carbs)
- **Protein:** Post-workout synergy

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Caffeine (high dose):** May blunt creatine's ergogenic effects (conflicting evidence)

### Drug Interactions
- **NSAIDs (Ibuprofen):** Combined with creatine may increase kidney stress (theoretical); stay hydrated
- **Diuretics:** Creatine may cause water retention; monitor

### Genetic Considerations
- **GAMT, GATM genes:** Creatine synthesis enzymes; deficiency requires creatine supplementation

### Special Notes
- **Water retention:** 1-2 kg weight gain from intramuscular water (not fat)
- **"Non-responders":** ~20-30% already have saturated creatine stores (often meat-eaters)
- **Hair loss myth:** No strong evidence linking creatine to hair loss

## Ashwagandha (KSM-66 & Sensoril)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Latin Name:** Withania somnifera
- **Active Compounds:** Withanolides (steroidal lactones)
- **Forms:**
  - **KSM-66®:** Root-only extract, 5% withanolides, full-spectrum, best studied
  - **Sensoril®:** Root + leaf extract, higher withaferin A, more sedating
  - **Standardized extracts:** 1.5-10% withanolides

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Traditional Ayurvedic herb

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **KSM-66:**
  - Stress/anxiety: 300-600 mg/day (5% withanolides)
  - Testosterone/strength: 600 mg/day
  - Cognitive function: 300 mg 2×/day
- **Sensoril:**
  - Stress/sleep: 125-250 mg/day (more sedating)
- **Duration:** 8-12 weeks for full benefits

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** >2,000 mg/kg (animal studies)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** >900 mg/day may cause GI upset, sedation
- **Thyroid stimulation:** May increase T3/T4 (beneficial for hypothyroid, caution for hyperthyroid)

### When to Take (Timing)
- **KSM-66:** Morning or afternoon (can be energizing for some)
- **Sensoril:** Evening (more sedating)
- **With or without food:** Withfood may reduce GI upset

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Magnesium:** Synergistic for stress, sleep
- **Rhodiola:** Adaptogen synergy (stress adaptation)
- **Black pepper (piperine):** May enhance absorption

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Sedatives:** May enhance sedative effects
- **Immunosuppressants:** Ashwagandha may stimulate immune system (theoretical)

### Drug Interactions
- **Thyroid medications:** Ashwagandha may increase T3/T4; monitor thyroid function
- **Sedatives/Benzodiazepines:** May enhance sedative effects
- **Immunosuppressants:** May counteract (theoretical)
- **Diabetes medications:** May lower blood sugar; monitor

### Genetic Considerations
- **COMT Val158Met (Met/Met - "worrier"):** May benefit more from ashwagandha's anxiolytic effects
- **Thyroid genes (TPO, TG):** Monitor if thyroid issues (ashwagandha can stimulate thyroid)

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ HYPERTHYROIDISM:** May worsen condition (thyroid-stimulating)
- **⚠️ PREGNANCY:** Traditionally considered abortifacient; AVOID during pregnancy
- **⚠️ AUTOIMMUNE DISEASES:** May stimulate immune system (MS, lupus, RA); use caution

## Coenzyme Q10 (Ubiquinone & Ubiquinol)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Ubiquinone (CoQ10):** Oxidized form, requires conversion to ubiquinol
- **Ubiquinol:** Reduced form, active antioxidant, 8× better absorption
- **Function:** Mitochondrial electron transport chain (ATP production), antioxidant

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Body synthesizes CoQ10

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General health (ubiquinol):** 100-200 mg/day
- **Statin users (CoQ10 depletion):** 100-300 mg/day ubiquinol
- **Heart failure:** 100-300 mg/day ubiquinol
- **Fertility (male):** 200-300 mg/day ubiquinol
- **Migraines:** 300 mg/day ubiquinone or 150 mg ubiquinol
- **Parkinson's (neuroprotection):** 300-1,200 mg/day

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Very high
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** Up to 1,200 mg/day studied safely; may cause GI upset

### When to Take (Timing)
- **With largest meal (fat-containing):** Fat-soluble, requires dietary fat for absorption

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin E:** Synergistic antioxidant
- **Omega-3:** Synergistic for cardiovascular health
- **PQQ:** Mitochondrial synergy
- **Dietary fat:** 10-15g fat enhances absorption

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **None significant**

### Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** CoQ10 structurally similar to Vitamin K; may reduce anticoagulant effect; monitor INR
- **Statins:** Statins deplete CoQ10 → muscle pain, fatigue; supplementation strongly recommended
- **Blood pressure medications:** CoQ10 may lower blood pressure; monitor

### Genetic Considerations
- **COQ2-COQ9 genes:** Mutations cause CoQ10 deficiency syndromes
- **APOE4:** May benefit from CoQ10 for neuroprotection

### Special Notes
- **Ubiquinol vs Ubiquinone:** Ubiquinol preferred for >40 years old, statin users, poor absorbers (8× better absorption)

## NAC (N-Acetyl Cysteine)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Chemical Name:** N-Acetyl-L-Cysteine
- **Precursor to:** Glutathione (master antioxidant)
- **Medical Use:** Acetaminophen overdose antidote, mucolytic (breaks up mucus)

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Cysteine is conditionally essential

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **Antioxidant support:** 600-1,200 mg/day
- **Liver detox:** 600-1,800 mg/day
- **Respiratory health (mucus):** 600-1,200 mg/day
- **Mental health (OCD, trichotillomania):** 1,200-2,400 mg/day
- **PCOS:** 1,200-1,800 mg/day
- **Acetaminophen overdose:** 140 mg/kg loading dose (medical emergency)

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** >4,000 mg/kg
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** >2,400 mg/day may cause nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
- **Sulfur content:** May cause "rotten egg" burps (take with food)

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Morning or evening:** With food to reduce GI upset
- **Away from exercise:** May blunt exercise-induced adaptations (antioxidant interference)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin C:** Synergistic antioxidant
- **Selenium:** Supports glutathione peroxidase (uses glutathione)
- **Glycine + Glutamine:** Complete glutathione synthesis

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Nitroglycerin:** NAC may enhance nitrate tolerance (medical supervision)

### Drug Interactions
- **Nitroglycerin:** May cause hypotension; medical supervision required
- **Chemotherapy:** High-dose antioxidants may reduce efficacy; avoid during treatment
- **Activated charcoal:** Reduces NAC absorption (relevant for acetaminophen overdose protocol)

### Genetic Considerations
- **GSTM1/GSTT1 null genotypes:** Glutathione-S-transferase deficiency; may benefit from NAC

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ BLUNTS EXERCISE ADAPTATIONS:** High-dose antioxidants may reduce exercise-induced muscle adaptations; avoid immediately post-workout

## Melatonin

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Chemical Name:** N-acetyl-5-methoxytryptamine
- **Forms:** Immediate-release (IR), Extended-release (ER/XR), Sublingual
- **Function:** Circadian rhythm regulation, sleep-wake cycle, antioxidant

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Hormone produced by pineal gland

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **Sleep onset (jet lag, shift work):** 0.3-5 mg, 30-60 min before desired sleep time
- **Insomnia:** 1-10 mg (start low, titrate up)
- **Extended-release (sleep maintenance):** 2-5 mg
- **Elderly (age-related decline):** 1-3 mg
- **Note:** "Less is more" - 0.3-1 mg often as effective as higher doses, fewer side effects

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Very high (>800 mg/kg)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** >10 mg may cause next-day grogginess, vivid dreams/nightmares

### When to Take (Timing)
- **30-60 minutes before desired sleep time** (circadian rhythm cue)
- **Jet lag:** Take at destination bedtime for 3-5 days
- **Shift work:** Take before daytime sleep (night shift workers)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Magnesium glycinate:** Synergistic for sleep
- **L-theanine:** Relaxation synergy
- **Glycine:** Sleep quality synergy

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Caffeine:** Antagonizes melatonin's sleep effects
- **Blue light (screens):** Suppresses endogenous melatonin production

### Drug Interactions
- **Sedatives/Benzodiazepines:** Additive sedative effects
- **Blood thinners (Warfarin):** Melatonin may increase bleeding risk; monitor INR
- **Immunosuppressants:** Melatonin may stimulate immune system
- **Blood pressure medications:** Melatonin may lower blood pressure; monitor
- **Diabetes medications:** Melatonin may affect glucose metabolism; monitor

### Genetic Considerations
- **CYP1A2 (rs762551):** Slow metabolizers may have prolonged melatonin effects, need lower doses
- **MTNR1A/MTNR1B genes (Melatonin receptors):** Variants affect melatonin sensitivity, diabetes risk

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ NEXT-DAY GROGGINESS:** More common with >5 mg doses or extended-release in poor metabolizers
- **⚠️ CHILDREN:** Use only under medical supervision; affects puberty hormones
- **⚠️ PREGNANCY:** Safety not established; avoid
- **⚠️ AUTOIMMUNE DISEASES:** May stimulate immune system; use caution

## Curcumin (Turmeric Extract)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Latin Name:** Curcuma longa
- **Active Compounds:** Curcuminoids (curcumin, demethoxycurcumin, bisdemethoxycurcumin)
- **Bioavailability Issue:** Poor absorption (~1%), rapid metabolism
- **Enhanced Forms:**
  - **BCM-95® (Bio-Curcumax):** Curcumin + turmeric essential oils, 7× better absorption
  - **Meriva® (Phytosome):** Curcumin + phosphatidylcholine, 29× better bioavailability
  - **Longvida®:** Solid lipid curcumin particle, crosses blood-brain barrier
  - **CurcuWIN®:** Water-dispersible curcumin, 46× better absorption
  - **BioTurm®:** **60× better bioavailability** (2025 research - most potent)
  - **Curcumin + Piperine (black pepper extract):** 20× better absorption (older method)

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Traditional spice, GRAS status (Generally Recognized As Safe)

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **Standard curcumin (95% curcuminoids):** 500-2,000 mg/day (low bioavailability, limited effect)
- **BCM-95®:** 500-1,000 mg/day
- **Meriva® (Phytosome):** 1,000-2,000 mg/day (200-400 mg curcumin equivalent)
- **Longvida®:** 400-800 mg/day (cognitive, brain health)
- **BioTurm®:** 50-100 mg/day (due to 60× absorption)
- **Anti-inflammatory:** 500-1,000 mg enhanced form, 2×/day
- **Osteoarthritis:** 1,000-1,500 mg enhanced form/day
- **Clinical Evidence (2024-2025):**
  - [Frontiers in Pharmacology (2025)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1509045/full): BioTurm® showed 60× bioavailability vs standard curcumin
  - [Frontiers in Nutrition (2025)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1572376/full): High-altitude study showed curcumin supplementation improved oxygen metabolism

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** >2,000 mg/kg (very safe)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **EFSA:** Up to 3 mg/kg bodyweight safe (210 mg for 70kg person for standard curcumin)
- **High doses:** >8,000 mg/day may cause GI upset, diarrhea, nausea
- **Note:** Enhanced bioavailability forms allow lower doses with better effects

### When to Take (Timing)
- **With largest meal (fat-containing):** Fat-soluble, requires dietary fat for absorption
- **Divided doses:** 2-3× per day (short half-life, ~6-7 hours)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Black pepper (piperine 5-10 mg):** 20× absorption boost (inhibits glucuronidation)
- **Healthy fats (omega-3, olive oil):** Enhance absorption
- **Quercetin:** Synergistic anti-inflammatory
- **Ginger:** Synergistic anti-inflammatory

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Iron supplements:** Curcumin chelates iron; separate by 2-4 hours

### Drug Interactions
- **⚠️ Blood thinners (Warfarin, aspirin):** Curcumin has antiplatelet effects; may increase bleeding risk; monitor INR
- **⚠️ Chemotherapy:** May interfere with certain chemotherapy drugs (consult oncologist)
- **Diabetes medications:** May lower blood sugar; monitor glucose
- **Stomach acid reducers (PPIs):** Curcumin reduces stomach acid production; may be synergistic or reduce absorption

### Genetic Considerations
- **IL1B, IL6, TNF-α promoter polymorphisms:** High inflammatory genotypes may benefit more from curcumin
- **UGT1A1 (Gilbert's syndrome):** Curcumin inhibits UGT enzymes; may increase bilirubin (usually benign)

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ GALLSTONES:** Curcumin stimulates bile production; avoid if gallbladder disease
- **⚠️ PREGNANCY:** Safety not established; avoid high doses
- **⚠️ BLEEDING DISORDERS:** Antiplatelet effects; avoid before surgery (discontinue 2 weeks prior)

### Evidence Quality
- **STRONG CLINICAL EVIDENCE** for anti-inflammatory effects, osteoarthritis pain reduction
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for metabolic syndrome, diabetes management
- **EMERGING EVIDENCE** for cognitive function (Longvida®), depression (adjunct)
- **Key Limitation:** Most older studies used poor bioavailability forms; 2024-2025 research focuses on enhanced formulations

## Berberine

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Source:** Extracted from Berberis aristata (Indian Barberry), Coptis chinensis (Goldthread), Hydrastis canadensis (Goldenseal)
- **Active Compound:** Berberine HCl (hydrochloride) - most common supplement form
- **Enhanced Forms:**
  - **Dihydroberberine (DHB):** 5× better absorption, converts to berberine in body
  - **Liposomal berberine:** Enhanced bioavailability
- **Function:** AMPK activator (cellular energy sensor), glucose metabolism, gut microbiome modulator

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Traditional Chinese medicine herb

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **Blood sugar management:** 500 mg, 3×/day (1,500 mg total), before meals
- **Lipid management:** 500 mg, 2-3×/day (1,000-1,500 mg total)
- **PCOS:** 500 mg, 3×/day
- **Weight management:** 500 mg, 3×/day
- **Gut health (SIBO):** 500 mg, 3×/day, 4-6 weeks

- **Clinical Evidence (2024-2025):**
  - [Journal of Diabetes & Metabolic Disorders (2025)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09731296241287592): RCT comparing berberine to metformin showed **equal efficacy** for fasting blood glucose reduction; berberine showed **better lipid profile improvement** (LDL, triglycerides)
  - Meta-analysis: Berberine reduces HbA1c by 0.7-1.0%, similar to metformin

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** ~27 mg/kg (IV in rats); oral LD50 much higher (~1,000-2,000 mg/kg)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** >2,000 mg/day may cause GI distress (cramping, diarrhea, constipation, gas)
- **Note:** GI side effects common at therapeutic doses (500 mg 3×/day); start low, titrate up

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Before meals (3× per day):** Optimizes glucose metabolism, reduces GI upset
- **Spacing:** Divide total daily dose into 2-3 doses due to short half-life (4-6 hours)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Probiotics:** Berberine alters gut microbiome; probiotics may reduce GI side effects
- **Chromium:** Synergistic for blood sugar control
- **Alpha-lipoic acid:** Synergistic for metabolic syndrome
- **Milk thistle (silymarin):** May protect liver during long-term use

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **None significant**

### Drug Interactions
- **⚠️ METFORMIN (Diabetes drug):** Additive blood sugar lowering; may cause hypoglycemia; monitor glucose
- **⚠️ CYP3A4 substrates:** Berberine inhibits CYP3A4 enzyme (metabolizes ~50% of drugs); may increase drug levels:
  - Statins (atorvastatin, simvastatin) → increased statin levels, muscle pain risk
  - Cyclosporine (immunosuppressant) → increased levels
  - Many others - consult pharmacist
- **⚠️ P-glycoprotein inhibition:** May increase absorption of certain drugs
- **Antibiotics:** Berberine has antimicrobial properties; may be synergistic or antagonistic depending on antibiotic

### Genetic Considerations
- **APOE4:** Berberine may improve lipid metabolism in APOE4 carriers
- **TCF7L2 (diabetes risk gene):** May benefit from berberine's glucose-lowering effects
- **CYP3A4 variants:** Poor metabolizers may have higher berberine levels, increased side effects

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ PREGNANCY & BREASTFEEDING:** May cross placenta, found in breast milk; can cause kernicterus in newborns; **AVOID**
- **⚠️ HYPOGLYCEMIA RISK:** Especially with diabetes medications; monitor blood sugar
- **⚠️ LONG-TERM USE:** Some concerns about B vitamin depletion (similar to metformin); consider B-complex supplementation
- **⚠️ CHILDREN:** Not recommended (safety data limited)

### Evidence Quality
- **STRONG CLINICAL EVIDENCE** for blood glucose reduction (comparable to metformin)
- **STRONG EVIDENCE** for lipid profile improvement (LDL, triglycerides)
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for PCOS, weight loss, NAFLD (non-alcoholic fatty liver disease)
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for gut health (SIBO, dysbiosis)
- **Key Advantage:** "Nature's Metformin" without prescription; 2025 head-to-head trials confirm equivalence

- **Muscle mass (sarcopenia):** 15 g/day + resistance training
- **Mixed types (I, II, III):** 10-20 g/day
- **Clinical Evidence (2024-2025):**

  - [PMC 2025 Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11734850/): Mixed collagen (Types I, II, III) improved osteoarthritis symptoms; Type II showed superior cartilage support

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Not applicable (food protein)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Skin/general:** Anytime, with or without food
- **Joint health (before activity):** 30-60 min before exercise (some studies show benefit)
- **Muscle synthesis:** With resistance training (post-workout)
- **Empty stomach vs with food:** Conflicting evidence; consistency more important than timing

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin C (500-1,000 mg):** **CRITICAL** cofactor for collagen synthesis (hydroxylation of proline/lysine)
- **Glycine, Proline, Lysine:** Amino acids in collagen (often already in collagen supplements)
- **Copper, Zinc:** Cofactors for collagen cross-linking
- **Hyaluronic acid:** Synergistic for skin hydration

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **None significant**

### Drug Interactions
- **Minimal:** No significant drug interactions

### Genetic Considerations
- **COL1A1/COL1A2 genes:** Variants affect collagen production, bone density, injury risk; may benefit from supplementation
- **MMP genes (Matrix Metalloproteinases):** Affect collagen breakdown; high MMP activity may need higher collagen intake

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ ALLERGIES:** Marine collagen (fish allergy), bovine (beef allergy), chicken (poultry allergy)
- **⚠️ KIDNEY DISEASE:** High protein intake; consult physician

### Evidence Quality
- **STRONG CLINICAL EVIDENCE** for skin health (hydration, elasticity, wrinkles) - 2024 long-term RCT confirms **sustained benefits**
- **MODERATE-STRONG EVIDENCE** for joint health (osteoarthritis pain, stiffness)
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for bone density (postmenopausal women)
- **EMERGING EVIDENCE** for muscle mass (with resistance training), gut health
- **Key Insight:** Type specificity matters - Type I/III for skin, Type II for joints; mixed formulations provide broad benefits

## Probiotics (Multi-Strain Formulations)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Definition:** Live microorganisms that confer health benefits
- **Common Strains:**
  - **Lactobacillus:** L. rhamnosus, L. acidophilus, L. plantarum, L. casei, L. reuteri
  - **Bifidobacterium:** B. longum, B. bifidum, B. lactis, B. breve
  - **Saccharomyces boulardii:** Beneficial yeast (probiotic, not bacteria)
  - **Streptococcus thermophilus**
  - **Bacillus coagulans (Lactobacillus sporogenes):** Spore-forming, shelf-stable
- **Multi-Strain vs Single-Strain:** 2024-2025 research shows **multi-strain superior** for gut diversity
- **CFU (Colony Forming Units):** Measure of viable bacteria (5-100 billion CFU typical range)

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Emerging field

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **General gut health:** 5-10 billion CFU multi-strain
- **Digestive issues (IBS, bloating):** 10-50 billion CFU, 2-3 strains
- **Post-antibiotic restoration:** 50-100 billion CFU multi-strain, 4-8 weeks
- **Immune support:** 10-20 billion CFU (L. rhamnosus, B. lactis)
- **Mental health (gut-brain axis):** 10-20 billion CFU (L. helveticus, B. longum)
- **Vaginal health (women):** L. rhamnosus + L. reuteri, 10 billion CFU
- **Clinical Evidence (2024-2025):**
  - [MDPI Nutrients (2024-2025 study)](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/17/17/2886): **Multi-strain probiotics showed 70% symptom improvement** vs **45% for single-strain** in IBS patients
  - Meta-analysis: Multi-strain formulations (≥3 strains) more effective for gut diversity restoration than single strains

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Not applicable (live organisms)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** Up to 450 billion CFU studied safely (short-term)
- **Side effects:** Initial gas, bloating (first 1-2 weeks as gut adjusts); die-off reaction possible

### When to Take (Timing)
- **With or just before meals:** Food buffers stomach acid, improves survival to intestines
- **Morning or evening:** Consistency more important than timing
- **During antibiotics:** 2-3 hours APART from antibiotic dose (antibiotics kill probiotics)
- **Post-antibiotic:** Start immediately after antibiotic course ends, continue 4-8 weeks

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Prebiotics (fiber):** Feed probiotics - inulin, FOS (fructooligosaccharides), GOS (galactooligosaccharides)
- **Fermented foods:** Synergistic (yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi)
- **Polyphenols:** Support beneficial bacteria growth

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Antibiotics (simultaneous):** Antibiotics kill probiotics; separate by 2-3 hours
- **High heat (>120°F/49°C):** Kills bacteria; avoid hot beverages immediately after

### Drug Interactions
- **Immunosuppressants:** Probiotics stimulate immune system; theoretical risk in severely immunocompromised (transplant patients); consult physician
- **Antibiotics:** Reduce probiotic efficacy; separate doses

### Genetic Considerations
- **FUT2 gene (Secretor status):** Non-secretors have different gut microbiome; may respond differently to probiotics
- **LCT gene (Lactose intolerance):** Lactose-intolerant individuals may benefit from L. acidophilus, B. lactis

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ IMMUNOCOMPROMISED:** Risk of systemic infection (rare); avoid in severely immunocompromised, critically ill
- **⚠️ CENTRAL LINE/CATHETER:** Risk of bacterial translocation; avoid probiotics if central line present
- **⚠️ SHELF-STABLE VS REFRIGERATED:** Some strains require refrigeration; check label

### Evidence Quality
- **STRONG CLINICAL EVIDENCE** for antibiotic-associated diarrhea prevention
- **MODERATE-STRONG EVIDENCE** for IBS symptom improvement (multi-strain > single-strain per 2024-2025 research)
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for immune support, vaginal health, mental health (gut-brain axis)
- **EMERGING EVIDENCE** for metabolic health, weight management
- **Key Insight:** **Multi-strain probiotics (3+ strains) consistently outperform single-strain** in 2024-2025 trials (70% vs 45% symptom improvement)

- **NMN/NR (NAD+ precursors):** Synergistic for longevity pathways (SIRT1 activation requires NAD+)
- **Piperine (black pepper):** May improve absorption

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **None significant**

### Drug Interactions
- **⚠️ Blood thinners (Warfarin, aspirin):** Resveratrol has antiplatelet effects; may increase bleeding risk
- **CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2D6 substrates:** Resveratrol inhibits these enzymes; theoretical drug interactions (not well-studied clinically)
- **Immunosuppressants:** May modulate immune system; theoretical interaction

### Genetic Considerations
- **SIRT1 gene variants:** May affect response to resveratrol (SIRT1 is primary target)
- **CYP1A2 variants:** Affect resveratrol metabolism
- **APOE4:** Theoretical benefit for Alzheimer's risk (anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective mechanisms)

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ BLEEDING RISK:** Avoid before surgery (discontinue 2 weeks prior)
- **⚠️ HORMONE-SENSITIVE CONDITIONS:** Resveratrol has weak estrogenic activity; use caution in breast cancer, endometriosis

### Evidence Quality
- **WEAK HUMAN EVIDENCE** for longevity, anti-aging, disease prevention - **2024-2025 systematic reviews conclude neutral clinical effects**
- **STRONG MECHANISTIC EVIDENCE** (in vitro, animal studies) - SIRT1 activation, mitochondrial function, but **DOES NOT TRANSLATE TO HUMANS** at achievable oral doses
- **WEAK-MODERATE EVIDENCE** for cardiovascular health (blood pressure, lipids) - small effect sizes
- **Key Limitation:** **Poor bioavailability is the primary issue** - trans-resveratrol rapidly metabolized to inactive forms; even high doses (1,000+ mg) achieve low blood levels; **human trials consistently fail to replicate animal study benefits**

- **EMERGING EVIDENCE** for energy, fatigue, cardiovascular health
- **Key Limitation:** Most human studies are **small (n=10-20)**, short-term (8-24 weeks); need larger RCTs to confirm cognitive and mitochondrial benefits; **mechanistic evidence strong**, but clinical translation still being established

## Ginkgo Biloba

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Latin Name:** Ginkgo biloba
- **Active Compounds:** Flavonoids (24%), Terpene lactones (6%) - ginkgolides, bilobalide
- **Standardized Extract:** EGb 761® (most studied), 24/6 standardization (24% flavone glycosides, 6% terpene lactones)
- **Function:** Vasodilator (improves blood flow), antioxidant, neuroprotection

- **No official RDI:** Traditional herbal medicine

- **Intermittent claudication (circulation):** 120-160 mg/day
- **Tinnitus, vertigo:** 120-160 mg/day

- **Clinical Evidence (2024-2025):**

- **LD50:** Very high
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Divided doses:** 120 mg 2×/day (morning + afternoon) OR 240 mg once daily
- **With or without food:** Absorption not significantly affected

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Bacopa Monnieri:** Synergistic for cognitive function
- **Omega-3 (DHA):** Synergistic for brain health
- **B vitamins:** Support cognitive function

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **None significant**

### Drug Interactions
- **⚠️ Blood thinners (Warfarin, aspirin, clopidogrel):** Ginkgo has antiplatelet effects; may increase bleeding risk; **monitor INR**, avoid before surgery (discontinue 2 weeks prior)
- **⚠️ SSRIs (Antidepressants):** Rare reports of serotonin syndrome; use caution
- **⚠️ Seizure threshold-lowering drugs:** Ginkgotoxin in contaminated extracts may increase seizure risk (rare with standardized extracts)
- **CYP2C19, CYP3A4 substrates:** Ginkgo may induce these enzymes; theoretical drug interactions

### Genetic Considerations
- **APOE4:** May benefit from Ginkgo's neuroprotective effects (Alzheimer's risk reduction)
- **CYP2C19 variants:** May affect Ginkgo metabolism

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ BLEEDING DISORDERS:** Antiplatelet effects; avoid before surgery (discontinue 14 days prior)
- **⚠️ SEIZURE DISORDERS:** Rare reports of seizures (likely from ginkgotoxin contamination); use pharmaceutical-grade extracts only
- **⚠️ PREGNANCY:** Safety not established; avoid

### Evidence Quality
- **MODERATE-STRONG CLINICAL EVIDENCE** for mild cognitive impairment / dementia - **2024-2025 meta-analysis confirms significant benefits at 240 mg/day for ≥24 weeks**
- **WEAK EVIDENCE** for prevention in healthy individuals - **does NOT enhance cognition or lower dementia risk** in those without impairment
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for intermittent claudication (circulation), tinnitus
- **Key Insight:** Ginkgo **improves where impairment exists**, not for "brain boosting" in healthy populations; requires **long-term use (6+ months)** for full cognitive benefits

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Chemical Name:** 3,3′,4′,5,7-pentahydroxyflavone (flavonoid polyphenol)

- **LD50:** >2,000 mg/kg (animal studies)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** Up to 1,500 mg/day studied safely; >1,000 mg may cause headache, nausea

### When to Take (Timing)
- **With food:** Fat-soluble flavonoid, better absorption with meals
- **Divided doses:** 500 mg 2×/day OR 1,000 mg once daily

- **Resveratrol:** Synergistic polyphenol, may extend quercetin half-life

- **None significant**

### Genetic Considerations
- **IL1B, IL6, TNF-α promoter polymorphisms:** High inflammatory genotypes may benefit more from quercetin
- **CYP3A4 variants:** May affect quercetin metabolism

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ KIDNEY DISEASE:** High doses (>1,000 mg) may worsen kidney function; use caution
- **⚠️ PREGNANCY/BREASTFEEDING:** Safety not established; avoid high doses

### Evidence Quality
- **STRONG CLINICAL EVIDENCE** for anti-inflammatory and immune support - **2024-2025 studies confirm IgE suppression, cytokine modulation**
- **MODERATE-STRONG EVIDENCE** for COVID-19 adjunct therapy (viral clearance, symptom reduction)
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for allergies (natural antihistamine), cardiovascular health
- **Key Insight:** **Phytosome or liposomal forms (20× bioavailability) required for clinical efficacy**; standard quercetin poorly absorbed

## Glucosamine & Chondroitin

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Glucosamine:**
  - **Glucosamine Sulfate (preferred):** 2KCl or NaCl salt, most studied
  - **Glucosamine HCl:** Hydrochloride form, no sulfate (less effective)
  - **N-Acetyl Glucosamine (NAG):** Different mechanism, less studied for joints
- **Chondroitin Sulfate:** Glycosaminoglycan, component of cartilage matrix
- **Function:** Cartilage building blocks, anti-inflammatory, chondroprotective

### Recommended Daily Intake (RDI)
- **No official RDI:** Body synthesizes glucosamine from glucose + glutamine

### Optimal Daily Intake (Evidence-Based)
- **Osteoarthritis (knee, hip):** **Glucosamine Sulfate 1,500 mg/day + Chondroitin Sulfate 1,200 mg/day** (divided into 2-3 doses)
- **Alternative dosing:** 500 mg glucosamine + 400 mg chondroitin, 3×/day
- **Duration:** **Minimum 6-12 months** for structural benefits (slow-acting); symptom relief may begin at 4-8 weeks
- **Clinical Evidence (2024-2025):**
  - [MDPI Nutrients (2025 Systematic Review)](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/17/13/2093): **146 studies reviewed**, nearly 60% RCTs; **over 90% reported positive outcomes** for osteoarthritis and joint pain; **minimal adverse effects**
  - [PMC 2025 Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12250884/): Glucosamine-chondroitin combination showed **2-year joint space narrowing reduction** (mean difference 0.10 mm, p<0.05) - **structural disease-modifying effect**
  - **Synergistic Effect:** Combination therapy **more effective than either alone** - joint space narrowing in combination group was **half that of placebo** after 2 years
  - **Dosing Confirmation:** 1,500 mg glucosamine + 1,200 mg chondroitin daily supported by evidence

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Very high (>10,000 mg/kg)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** Up to 3,000 mg/day glucosamine studied safely
- **Side effects:** Mild GI upset (nausea, bloating, diarrhea) most common

### When to Take (Timing)
- **With meals:** Reduces GI upset (take with breakfast + dinner, OR breakfast + lunch + dinner)
- **Divided doses:** 2-3 doses per day (better absorption than single large dose)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Vitamin C:** Cofactor for collagen synthesis (synergistic for cartilage repair)
- **MSM (Methylsulfonylmethane):** Synergistic for joint pain, anti-inflammatory
- **Collagen Type II:** Synergistic for cartilage support
- **Boswellia:** Anti-inflammatory synergy

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **None significant**

### Drug Interactions
- **⚠️ Warfarin (Blood thinner):** Glucosamine may enhance anticoagulant effect (case reports); **monitor INR**
- **Diabetes medications:** Glucosamine theoretically may affect blood sugar (conflicting evidence); monitor glucose

### Genetic Considerations
- **COL2A1 gene (Type II collagen):** Variants affect cartilage integrity; may benefit from supplementation
- **GDF5 gene:** Osteoarthritis susceptibility gene; may respond better to glucosamine

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ SHELLFISH ALLERGY:** Glucosamine derived from shellfish exoskeletons (chitin); **may cause allergic reaction** - use vegetarian glucosamine (fermented corn)
- **⚠️ DIABETES:** Monitor blood sugar (theoretical concern, but human studies show no significant effect)
- **⚠️ ASTHMA:** Rare reports of exacerbation (theoretical); use caution

### Evidence Quality
- **STRONG CLINICAL EVIDENCE** for osteoarthritis symptom relief - **2025 systematic review: 90%+ positive outcomes** across 146 studies
- **MODERATE-STRONG EVIDENCE** for disease-modifying effect (slowing joint space narrowing) - **2025 study shows structural benefits over 2 years**
- **Combination > Monotherapy:** Glucosamine + Chondroitin **synergistic**, more effective than either alone
- **Key Insight:** **Long-term therapy required (6-12 months)** for full structural benefits; shellfish-free options available for allergies

- **LD50:** >17,500 mg/kg (very safe)

- **Molybdenum:** Cofactor for sulfur metabolism

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **None significant**

### Drug Interactions
- **Minimal:** No significant drug interactions reported

### Genetic Considerations
- **SOD2 (Superoxide Dismutase 2):** Variants affecting oxidative stress may benefit from MSM's antioxidant effects

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ SULFUR SENSITIVITY:** Rare; may cause allergic reaction in those sensitive to sulfa drugs (unrelated chemically, but caution advised)

### Evidence Quality
- **MODERATE-STRONG CLINICAL EVIDENCE** for knee/joint pain - **2023-2024 RCTs confirm symptom improvement**
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for anti-inflammatory effects, muscle recovery
- **WEAK-MODERATE EVIDENCE** for allergic rhinitis, skin/hair/nails
- **Key Insight:** **Well-tolerated and safe at therapeutic doses (3-6 g/day)**; often combined with glucosamine + chondroitin for synergistic joint benefits

## Alpha-Lipoic Acid (ALA)

### Scientific Name & Forms
- **Chemical Name:** 5-(1,2-dithiolan-3-yl)pentanoic acid
- **Forms:**
  - **R-Lipoic Acid (R-LA):** Natural, biologically active form

  - [Cochrane Review (2024)](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD012967.pub2/full): **Mixed results** - ALA "probably has little or no effect on symptoms of diabetic peripheral neuropathy" after 6 months (low-moderate certainty evidence)
  - [MDPI Biomedicines (2025)](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9059/13/2/438): 600 mg/day ALA for 6 months **reduced AGEs (advanced glycation end products)** in T2DM patients with neuropathy - **first human demonstration of AGE-reducing effect**
  - [PMC 2024 Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12191796/): ALA is "universal antioxidant" with powerful free radical scavenging, but **optimal dose, duration, and trial outcomes remain unclear**
  - **Key Limitation:** Symptom relief inconsistent, but **biochemical benefits (AGE reduction, antioxidant effects) confirmed**

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** >2,000 mg/kg
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** Up to 1,800 mg/day studied; may cause nausea, skin rash, hypoglycemia

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Empty stomach:** 30-60 min before meals (food reduces absorption by ~30%)
- **Divided doses:** 300 mg 2×/day OR 600 mg once daily (empty stomach)

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **B vitamins (especially Biotin, B1):** ALA may deplete biotin; supplement with B-complex
- **L-Carnitine:** Synergistic for mitochondrial function, energy metabolism
- **CoQ10:** Antioxidant synergy

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **Food (especially carbohydrates):** Reduces ALA absorption by 30-50%

### Drug Interactions
- **⚠️ Diabetes medications (Insulin, Metformin):** ALA lowers blood sugar; **may cause hypoglycemia**; monitor glucose closely
- **Thyroid medications (Levothyroxine):** ALA may reduce thyroid hormone levels; separate by 4+ hours
- **Chemotherapy:** High-dose antioxidants may reduce chemotherapy efficacy; avoid during treatment

### Genetic Considerations
- **APOE4:** Theoretical benefit for neuroprotection (Alzheimer's risk)
- **SOD2, GPX1 (Antioxidant enzyme genes):** Variants may benefit from ALA's antioxidant effects

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ HYPOGLYCEMIA RISK:** Especially with diabetes medications; monitor blood sugar
- **⚠️ BIOTIN DEPLETION:** Long-term high-dose ALA may deplete biotin; supplement with B-complex containing 300+ mcg biotin

### Evidence Quality
- **WEAK-MODERATE CLINICAL EVIDENCE** for diabetic neuropathy symptom relief - **2024 Cochrane review: little/no effect on symptoms**
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for AGE reduction, antioxidant effects - **2025 study confirms biochemical benefits**
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for blood sugar management (adjunct to medication)
- **Key Limitation:** **Symptom improvement inconsistent**, but **antioxidant and metabolic benefits confirmed**; "universal antioxidant" designation well-supported

  - [2024 Cancer Study](https://www.healthline.com/nutrition/reishi-mushroom-benefits): Reishi in cancer patients **reduced fatigue, anxiety, and depression**
  - [2024 Combination Trial](https://www.mindlabpro.com/blogs/nootropics/best-mushrooms-health): Reishi + Rhodiola rosea → **significant improvements in negative feelings, fatigue, overall mood** after 6 weeks
  - [Scientific Reports 2021 Mechanism Study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-94375-1): Reishi **promotes sleep through gut microbiota and serotonin pathways** (gut-brain axis mechanism)
  - **Key Insight:** Works via **gut microbiota modulation**, not direct brain receptors

### LD50 & Toxicity
- **LD50:** Very high (traditional food/medicine, >2,000 years of use)
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL):** Not established
- **High doses:** Up to 6,000 mg/day studied; may cause mild GI upset, dry mouth
- **Duration:** Safe for up to 12 months continuous use

### When to Take (Timing)
- **Evening (for sleep):** 1-2 hours before bed
- **Morning or evening (for stress/immune):** Consistency more important than timing
- **With food:** May reduce GI upset

### What to Take WITH (Co-Factors)
- **Other medicinal mushrooms:** Cordyceps, Lion's Mane (synergistic immune support)
- **Vitamin C:** Enhances polysaccharide absorption
- **Magnesium:** Synergistic for sleep

### What NOT to Take Together With (Antagonists)
- **None significant**

### Drug Interactions
- **Immunosuppressants:** Reishi stimulates immune system; may counteract immunosuppressants (transplant patients)
- **Blood thinners (Warfarin):** Reishi may have antiplatelet effects; monitor INR, avoid before surgery
- **Blood pressure medications:** Reishi may lower blood pressure; monitor
- **Diabetes medications:** May lower blood sugar; monitor glucose

### Genetic Considerations
- **HLA variants (Immune system genes):** Reishi's immunomodulatory effects may vary by HLA type
- **COMT Val158Met:** May benefit from Reishi's stress-reducing effects (especially Met/Met "worriers")

### Special Warnings
- **⚠️ BLEEDING DISORDERS:** Antiplatelet effects; avoid before surgery (discontinue 2 weeks prior)
- **⚠️ AUTOIMMUNE DISEASES:** May stimulate immune system; use caution in MS, lupus, RA
- **⚠️ PREGNANCY/BREASTFEEDING:** Safety not established; avoid

### Evidence Quality
- **MODERATE CLINICAL EVIDENCE** for sleep quality - **2024-2025 trials recruiting/ongoing**; mechanism (gut-brain axis) well-established
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for stress, fatigue, mood - **2024 combination trial confirms mood benefits**
- **MODERATE EVIDENCE** for immune support (cancer adjunct) - reduces anxiety, depression, fatigue in cancer patients
- **WEAK-MODERATE EVIDENCE** for cardiovascular health, blood sugar
- **Key Insight:** **Works via gut microbiota → serotonin pathway** (not direct sedative); requires **8-12 weeks** for full immune/stress benefits; **safe long-term use (up to 12 months)**
